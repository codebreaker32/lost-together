# Project Brief — "Lost Together" (working name)

OpenCV AI Competition 2026, powered by AWS · https://opencv26.devpost.com/
Status: concept agreed, nothing built yet. Last updated 2026-10-26 (rev. 3, after four independent reviews and the official rules).

> **Where this came from.** The team came up with the research question, the forest/predator scenario, the hiker storyline and the "living map" idea in a guided research dialogue. AI research agents, a judge panel and four reviewers suggested the architecture, the experiment design, the award strategy and the data choices. The team accepted the design changes.

---

## 1. Pitch

Lost hikers each see the forest only through their own camera, and something is hunting them. The same question applies to any group lost while a hazard moves toward it: wildfire, rising water, a bear. *Sharing a map built from what each hiker sees raised the share of hikers who escaped from **X%** to **Y%**. The benefit disappeared once fog cut visibility below **Z m** or the predator outpaced the hikers.* (X, Y and Z will be filled in from the results.)

## 2. Research question and pre-registered predictions

**Main question:** Do lost hikers who share a living map built from what each of them sees escape an uncertain predator more often than hikers acting alone? When does that stop being true?

Sub-questions:
1. **Where is the boundary?** Across predator speed and sensing range, where does sharing stop helping? The answer is a phase diagram.
2. **What does imperfect vision cost?** How much of the gap between vision and perfect perception does a shared map recover? Gap recovered = (V_share − V_solo) / (P_share − V_solo), where V is vision and P is perfect perception.
3. **Does a vision-triggered agent decide well?** Does it switch from "enjoying" to "escaping" at the right time, with few false alarms?

**Predictions.** Commit these with a timestamp before the main runs, and report every one whether it holds or fails:
- **H1.** Sharing beats solo on the primary outcome at the "benefit" anchor cell (the paired confidence interval excludes 0).
- **H2.** The benefit shrinks to zero, or reverses, as predator speed ÷ hiker speed rises past about 1, and as sensing radius ÷ visibility rises.
- **H3.** The benefit survives subtracting the predator-off control, so it isn't just "more eyes find the exit faster".
- **H4.** Vision-only scores below the perfect-perception reference.

**What would falsify it:**
- The confidence interval includes 0 at every cell (no benefit).
- The benefit is positive everywhere (no boundary).
- The effect vanishes after the predator-off control (it was about finding the exit, not the predator).
- Vision equals the reference (perception isn't the bottleneck).

## 3. World model

**"Living map", defined once:** the shared map updates as new sightings arrive, and old predator evidence fades over time.

| Element | Rule (frozen before the main runs) |
|---|---|
| Hikers | N = 4. All start together at a trailhead, then drift apart while "enjoying". Each wears a head-mounted stereo pair with a 0.25 m baseline (the TartanGround/TartanAir rig). |
| Exits | Fixed number and placement per forest map. Exit distance is expressed as a ratio of the predator's sensing radius. |
| Predator | Speed ratio v_pred / v_hiker and sensing radius R_sense are the two phase-diagram axes. Miss probability is a 1-D sensitivity check. Main policy: pursue the nearest hiker it senses. Robustness check: ambush near an exit. State whether fog also limits the predator's sensing. |
| Group detection | Each hiker is detected independently, so a group of N is detected with probability 1 − p_miss^N. Robustness check: detect the group as one target. |
| Capture | A hiker within capture radius leaves the simulation. The predator spends handling time t_h, then resumes. No violence is depicted. |
| Group movement | A group forms at the moment of the escape decision, not at the start. Group speed = the slowest member's. Group decisions: majority vote. |
| Communication | Main study: global, instant sharing. Robustness check: share only within range r_comm. This is listed as a limitation. |
| Dead-reckoning | A step-counter/IMU-style prior: true increments plus a random-walk bias, generated by the simulator and fused weakly with visual odometry. |
| Episode | Ends at T_max, or when every hiker has escaped or been caught. Threat onset = the first step the predator is within R_sense of any hiker. |

## 4. Simulator: a "viewpoint graph" built from real-looking forest frames

A live Unreal simulator needs a GPU. This account's GPU quota requests were closed at 0, so that option is out. Plain replay of recorded sequences can't work either: the camera paths are fixed, so the hikers' actions can't change what they see. Instead, the simulator works the way indoor-navigation benchmarks do (the Room-to-Room / Matterport pattern):

- **Nodes** are camera poses from TartanGround forest/nature environments: ground robots, 10 Hz, 640×640 frames, a 12-camera 360° stereo rig with a 0.25 m baseline, and ground-truth depth. TartanAir V2 ForestEnv is the backup. **Edges** link consecutive frames, plus junctions where trajectories pass within about 1 m. The graph is built from ground truth on the simulator side only.
- **Actions:** a hiker *turns* (picks one of the view directions) or *walks* (takes an edge).
- **Predator, decoys and other hikers** are composited into the left and right frames as 2D cut-outs. Each gets disparity matching its depth, and occlusion is tested against the ground-truth depth.
- **Fog** uses the scattering model I = J·e^(−βd) + A·(1 − e^(−βd)) with ground-truth depth d. β is the fog variable.
- **Predator and evaluator** run on a 2D map derived from the ground-truth poses or TartanGround occupancy.
- **Perception is cached.** Stereo depth, odometry between adjacent nodes and ALIKED features for each (node, view, fog level) never change, so compute them once and store them in S3. The detector re-runs only on frames with a composited cut-out. Every detection still comes from pixels, and an episode costs milliseconds.

*Licence check:* frames sent to the LLM agent must not carry "no generative AI" terms. Check the TartanAir/TartanGround asset terms. The same issue applies to any Unreal/Fab assets.

## 5. Perception — OpenCV 5.0.0 (pip `opencv-python==5.0.0.93`)

| Step | OpenCV 5 API and settings |
|---|---|
| Stereo depth | `StereoSGBM`, MODE_SGBM_3WAY, numDisparities 96–128, blockSize 5, P1 = 8·cn·b², P2 = 32·cn·b², uniquenessRatio 10, speckle filter. Optional WLS filter from `ximgproc` (contrib wheel). Output is int16 ×16, so depth = f·B/(disp/16). Mask disp ≤ 1. |
| Odometry | `ptcloud` Odometry with `OdometryType_RGB_DEPTH`. `setCameraMatrix(K.astype(float32))` with f = 320, c = 320 (float64 is rejected). maxDepth about 20–30 m (the default 4 m is tuned for Kinect indoors). Scale maxTranslation/maxRotation to the step size. Depth in float32 metres, with invalid pixels set to 0. **The success flag is not a validity check:** gate each estimate with ALIKED/ORB matches + stereo 3D points + `solvePnPRansac`. |
| Fallback odometry (kill-switch) | Stereo feature visual odometry: keypoints + stereo depth + frame-to-frame `solvePnPRansac`. Still all OpenCV. |
| Detection (no training) | OpenCV Zoo YOLOX-s (Apache-2.0; COCO classes include bear, person, dog, horse, cow, but not deer) on the new DNN engine, falling back to the classic engine. Predator = bear-like animal. Decoys = deer, dog, dark rocks. Natural confusions provide real false cues, and fog lowers confidence on its own. |
| Visibility estimate | Contrast/dark-channel estimate per frame. Validate on real smoke/fog imagery (§10). |

**Gaps in released 5.0.0, and how this plan fills them:**
- There is no SLAM or structure-from-motion bundle adjustment; `stitching`'s `BundleAdjuster*` is for panoramas only. There is also no loop closure and no place recognition, and `PoseGraph`/`LevMarq` are C++-only. → GTSAM handles the pose graph.
- `OdometryType::RGB` exists, but it is photometric RGB-D odometry and still needs depth, so there is no monocular (image-only) odometry. → Stereo.
- `cv::slam::VisualOdometry` exists only on the unreleased 5.x branch. Don't depend on it.
- ALIKED/LightGlue weights aren't bundled, so supply the ONNX files and check their licences.
- Gotchas:
  - The 5.0.0 Python odometry sample is broken; use `cv.OdometryType_DEPTH`-style enum names.
  - HashTSDF wants float32 depth.
  - DNN engine constant names differ between 5.0.0 and 5.x, so check `dir(cv2.dnn)`.
- Keep GPL SLAM code out (ORB-SLAM3, COVINS, g2o's GPL parts).

**Requirements table for the write-up:**
- Each OpenCV 5 API → what it does here → % of per-frame time.
- Each AWS service → its role → cost per sweep.
- One sentence: "GTSAM only solves the pose graph; all pixel work is OpenCV 5."

## 6. Shared living map

- **Common frame:** every hiker is anchored to the shared trailhead start pose. Merging works *because* they share a start point; say so in the write-up.
- **Place recognition:**
  1. Build a visual-word vocabulary once, offline: k-means centres from ALIKED descriptors of a *different* environment, stored in `ANNIndex`. (Annoy indexes can't grow after building, so they aren't used for the live map.)
  2. Represent each keyframe as a bag-of-words histogram, and search keyframes with brute-force cosine similarity in numpy. A few thousand keyframes is trivial.
  3. Verify the top 3–5 candidates with LightGlue + `solvePnPRansac` using stereo depth, requiring at least 30–50 inliers.
- **Optimisation:** GTSAM 4.3.0 (aarch64 wheels, BSD licence) with `GncLMOptimizer` or Huber/Cauchy robust noise models. Forests alias badly (every trunk looks alike), so expect false loop closures. Use Pose3 factors, with covariance scaled by inlier count, plus weak dead-reckoning factors.
- **Map layers:** a 2D occupancy grid, plus a predator-risk heatmap with time decay (the "living" part).
- **Localisation report:** three rows on TartanGround ground truth: dead-reckoning only, visual odometry only, and fused.

## 7. Decisions, agent and human control

- **Rule-based planner (every step):** routes to the exit while avoiding risk. It gets the *same triggers and the same actions* as the LLM agent, including "look again when confidence is in [a, b]". Its thresholds are tuned on held-out seeds within a stated budget.
- **LLM hiker agent (Agentic Vision), as an active-perception loop:** it is called on uncertain events and can spend look-again actions:
  - turn to another view direction, or crop and zoom;
  - compare against an earlier frame;
  - query the shared map.

  Its tools are OpenCV 5 operations served by **one** OpenCV 5-native MCP server (none is maintained today), consumed by a Strands MCP client. That makes one layer, not two.
- **Model provider: self-hosted `qwen3-vl` (Apache-2.0; vision + tools) served by Ollama on an EC2 GPU in ap-south-1.** This keeps inference on AWS. Bedrock is unavailable on the `bhawna` account (0 tokens/day in ap-south-1, "Operation not allowed" in us-east-1). Local CPU inference is too slow to develop against (`qwen3-vl:4b` took 498 s per step), so develop agent logic against recorded or stubbed responses and make real calls only in GPU sessions. On the GPU, use a **g6e.xlarge** (L40S, 48 GB), the largest that fits the quota. **Chosen model: `qwen3-vl:30b`, thinking off.** In the 2026-10-26 smoke test it took about 2.1 s per step (decoding 182 tokens/s) against 4.7 s for the 8b, and both models completed the look-again loop in every message format. Tier C (about 2,000 calls) is therefore roughly 70 GPU-minutes, about $2.60. GPU spend is capped at **$10** ($2.235/h, about 4.5 h in total), so every GPU session follows start → run a batch → stop, with an automatic shutdown as a safety net. Groq is the fallback; its test results (2026-10-26) were:
  - **What works:** the model reads images, calls tools sensibly, and completes the full look-again loop in about 0.6 s per step.
  - **Rule 1:** Groq rejects images inside tool results ("content must be a string"). Return a text tool result, then send the new frame in a **follow-up user message**.
  - **Rule 2:** the model is poor at pixel coordinates. It asked to zoom outside a 256×256 frame, and later at the frame centre instead of the shape. So `look_closer` should take an **OpenCV detector candidate ID**, not coordinates: OpenCV finds regions, the model chooses.
  - **Rule 3:** call the provider through an OpenAI-compatible interface, so a model self-hosted on an AWS GPU can replace Groq with a config change.
  - The `gpt-oss` models are text-only. No Llama 4 vision models are available on this account.
  - **Current-tier limits:** 1,000 requests/day and about 7,000 input tokens/minute. One 256×256 image call is about 2.5k tokens, so roughly 2–3 image calls per minute. Tier C (Groq requests count against these limits) must be batched over days with cached responses, or the account upgraded.
- **Fairness and reproducibility:**
  - Simulation time freezes during each model call, and a fixed "think time" in simulated seconds is charged.
  - Real p50/p95 latency is reported separately.
  - Every prompt and response is logged and cached by input hash.
  - A subset is repeated k = 3 times to report run-to-run variance.
- **Human control:** a **ranger console** where a person approves or overrides an evacuation broadcast, plus an autonomy dial (*advise only* / *act*). Model calls have retries and timeouts, and every decision is traced.

## 8. Experiment design — three tiers, not a full factorial

The full cross (2 × 3 × 2 conditions × 5 swept parameters × seeds) would be about 37,500 cells, far beyond 8 vCPU. It is **not** run.

| Tier | What | Scale |
|---|---|---|
| **A — calibrated surrogate (phase diagram)** | Perception replaced by noise curves **measured from the real OpenCV pipeline** in the simulator: drift per metre, detection precision/recall vs. distance and fog, false-positive rate. Grid of speed ratio (7 levels) × sensing radius (7 levels), × cooperation (solo / share / group; optional split into "share geometry only" vs. "share sightings only"), × 200 paired seeds. Also: 1-D slices of fog, miss probability and false-cue rate at two anchor points; a **predator-off control**; robustness runs (ambush policy, other N and map size, comms range). | About 10⁵ episodes at under 1 s each, a few CPU-hours |
| **B — full pixels** | Three anchor cells (clear benefit / near the boundary / no benefit) × 3 cooperation conditions × 100 paired seeds, using the cached-perception simulator. **Validity check:** the surrogate's share-minus-solo difference must fall inside the 95% CI of the pixel runs. Report any disagreement. | 900 episodes |
| **C — rule-based vs. LLM** | Offline paired benchmark: log 300–500 escalation points (state snapshot + frames) from Tier B, have both policies decide on the identical set, and score against evaluator ground truth (McNemar test). Ablation: the LLM sees only the rule's scalar features, which separates reasoning from looking at pixels. Plus closed-loop LLM runs at 1–2 anchors × 30–50 paired seeds. The LLM is one point plotted against the rule's false-alarm vs. late-escape curve. | About 500–2,000 model calls |

Wording for the write-up: *"The phase diagram uses a perception model measured from our OpenCV pipeline and checked against full-pixel runs at three cells."*

**The perfect-perception reference** replaces "oracle". It keeps the same field of view, fog occlusion and cues, but gives perfect self-pose and perfect labels for cues in view, with no other knowledge of predator state. It runs through the same planner code. Call it a reference, not an upper bound. No LLM runs in this condition.

**Outcomes:**
- **Primary:** the fraction of hikers reaching an exit within T_max, analysed **per episode** (episodes are the unit, because hikers in one episode are correlated).
- **Secondary:**
  - an all-escaped indicator;
  - time to escape as censored data (Kaplan–Meier curves, restricted mean survival time);
  - closest approach.
- **Decision quality, defined in the evaluator:**
  - A *real cue* is a true cue with at least k visible pixels.
  - A *false-alarm escape* is an escape decision when no hiker was within d of the predator for W seconds.
  - *Decision-change rate* is the share of decision points where withholding the latest frame changes the action.

**Statistics:**
- **Common random numbers:** each seed fixes the forest, spawns, exits, predator start and cue schedule across conditions, with separate random streams for the predator, cues and noise.
- **Paired tests:** McNemar or paired bootstrap.
- **Phase diagram:** fitted as a logistic GAM of escape on cooperation × (speed ratio, radius ratio). Plot Δ = P(escape | share) − P(escape | solo) with the Δ = 0 contour and a bootstrap 95% band, and mask cells whose CI includes 0.
- **Multiple comparisons:** Holm-correct the headline contrasts.
- **Openness:** publish seeds and raw episode logs.

**Realism check:** report where the simulator's drift and detector precision/recall sit relative to real forest data (FoMo, FinnForest). Add one Tier A point with the noise set to the real-forest drift, to show whether the phase boundary depends on simulator realism.

## 11. Demo and UX

**Video storyboard (about 2 minutes, screen-recorded, captioned):**
1. **0–20 s:** hikers enjoying the forest, then a dark shape in the fog. Split-screen camera tiles with OpenCV detection boxes and confidence.
2. **20–60 s:** the **same seed run twice, side by side.** SOLO: a hiker is caught. SHARED MAP: the group escapes. The living map, risk heatmap and routes redraw live.
3. **60–90 s:** the agent's decision trace ("confidence 0.41 → looked again → 0.83 → escape") and the ranger console approving the broadcast.
4. **90–120 s:** the phase diagram, with one sentence on where cooperation stops helping.

**Replay viewer:** a static web page over the public S3 results that judges can open without an AWS account. It shows camera tiles, the map, decision traces and the solo-vs-shared toggle. Use a colour-blind-safe palette and keyboard navigation.

**README above the fold:** the pitch, a hero GIF, the phase diagram, the requirements table (§5), and a no-AWS local replay command.

## 14. Risks and responsible use

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Vision looks decorative | Ground-truth boundary; the surrogate is measured from the real pipeline and checked against pixel runs; real-data scores; three localisation rows. |
| Result is an artefact of predator parameters | Axes as ratios; fixed-parameter table; robustness runs; predator-off control. |
| LLM agent no better than the rules | Fair comparison (same triggers, actions and tuning budget); report honestly; the active-perception loop still meets the agentic criteria. |
| Bedrock or EC2 quotas never arrive | The Overall entry doesn't need the LLM. COOL fits on one 2xlarge. |
| **"Gratuitously violent" content** (judges can reject it) | No gore or attack animation; a caught hiker leaves the simulation; calm wording ("intercepted"). |
| Data used without rights | Licensed sources only, attributed in the report and video. Show only own renders or CC0/CC BY imagery in the video (it is licensed to OpenCV/AWS for any use). |
| Misrepresented results (rejection ground) | Pre-registered predictions; report every falsifier; state clearly that everything is simulated and which tier each figure comes from. |

Responsible-use statement in the write-up: everything is simulated and no real people are filmed. Least-privilege IAM, model calls bounded by timeouts and budgets, and the agent disclosed with its limits.
