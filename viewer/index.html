        How the hikers see and decide

        1. Each hiker has a camera

          Every hiker sees the forest through its own 640 × 640 frame: TartanGround forest imagery with the predator or a natural decoy composited at its true distance, and fog added with the scattering model.

        2. OpenCV 5 finds the predator

          YOLOX-s runs on the new cv2.dnn engine (ENGINE_NEW). On a full frame it finds the predator out to about 15 m.

        3. Look closer

          When a report is uncertain, the hiker re-runs the detector on a 3× zoom tile around it. That confirms or dismisses it out to about 20 to 30 m.

        4. One living map

          Hikers who share put every sighting on one map, and old evidence fades. A teammate's report makes a hiker scan with zoom.

        5. Decide, with a person in the loop

          A rule-based planner and an LLM agent (qwen3-vl on an AWS GPU, calling OpenCV 5 tools through an MCP server) decide when to escape. An escape order is an evacuation broadcast, so a ranger approves or overrides it.
